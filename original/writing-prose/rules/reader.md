# Reader rules

These rules hold for every surface except chat.
Each names one thing a reader can lack.
A reader who lacks it has to guess, however well the sentences are shaped, so judge these rules before the sentence-shape rules.

- `argument` [M] Give every verb and every noun that names an action its parts: who acts, on what, to where, and what shows it.
- `scope` [M] State a rule's condition and whose case it covers in the open, and give the case it excludes a rule of its own.
- `define-first` [M] Define a term or a phrase you coined before a sentence uses it as known: the definition with its condition first, then the rule.
- `relation` [M] Name how the parts of a list or a run of clauses combine, as in "in any of the following cases", and give each case one item once there are more than three.
- `fact-home` [M] Put a fact in the section whose question it answers, and move one that answers another section's question.
- `whole-sentence` [M] Give every sentence a subject and a verb, and make sure it parses one way only.
